# Core result (E3/E4/E5): does the graph-forecast stress test predict later detector performance?

Design frozen beforehand: `docs/e3e4-design.md` (one deviation, logged before the holdout was read).
Run once on 2026-11-21: `python -m gffs.e3e4 --data data/elliptic --literature results/literature_graph_v2.json --out results/`
(about 4 minutes). Raw output: `results/e3e4_elliptic.json`.

**Setup:**
- **Detectors:** 10 graph-only detectors, trained on steps up to the cutoff, with thresholds set at a 5% false-positive
  rate on the last 6 pre-cutoff steps.
- **Stress tests:**
  - **S0** recent data (real illicit transactions in the last 6 pre-cutoff steps);
  - **S1** graph forecast (top 10 motif pairs from the pre-2017 literature graph, ranked by `logreg_all`);
  - **S2** 10 uniform random pairs;
  - **S3** the 8 single motifs.
- **Each synthetic set:** 200 instances per pair, injected into the pre-cutoff graph.
- **Metrics:** E3 = Spearman ρ between stress-test recall and real later recall across the 10 detectors. E4 = mean absolute
  recall error. 95% intervals from 1,000 bootstrap resamples.

## 1. Against the frozen success criteria: **not met**

The criterion was: S1 has higher ρ than S0 **and** S2 (interval of the difference above 0), and a smaller error than S0
and S2, on 35–49 and on 43–49.

| Cutoff 34 window | S1 − S0: Δρ [95% CI] | S1 − S2: Δρ [95% CI] | S1 − S0: Δ error | S1 − S2: Δ error |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 35–49 (all) | −0.07 [−0.24, +0.10] | +0.00 [−0.04, +0.07] | +0.047 (worse) | +0.006 |
| 35–42 (before shutdown) | **−0.31** [−0.38, −0.04] | +0.07 [+0.02, +0.12] | +0.061 (worse) | +0.006 |
| 43–49 (after shutdown) | +0.13 [−0.05, +0.43] | +0.10 [−0.05, +0.17] | **−0.073** [−0.085, −0.056] | +0.001 |

**The forecast-chosen motif pairs (S1) are not reliably better than random motif pairs (S2).**
- **Ranking (E3):** S1 beats S2 with an interval above 0 in 2 of 7 windows (cutoff 34 before the shutdown, +0.07
  [+0.02, +0.12]; cutoff 24, +0.22 [+0.15, +0.24]). In both, every synthetic set ranks detectors poorly (ρ ≤ 0.57).
  In the post-shift windows, where synthetic sets work, S1 is no better than S2.
- **Calibration (E4):** S1 is never better than S2, and is slightly worse (+0.005 to +0.010) in several windows. The literature forecast picks pairs well *as a forecaster* (+0.241 AUC, see `RESEARCH_LOG.md`), but that does
not carry over into a better stress test.

## 2. What the stress tests do show

Spearman ρ with holdout recall, across 10 detectors (95% CI):

| Run, window | S0 recent | S1 forecast | S2 random pairs | S3 single motifs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cutoff 34, 35–49 | **+0.82** [+0.66, +0.92] | +0.75 | +0.75 | +0.61 |
| cutoff 34, 35–42 (before) | **+0.88** [+0.76, +0.93] | +0.57 | +0.49 | +0.43 |
| cutoff 34, 43–49 (after) | +0.77 [+0.44, +0.87] | **+0.91** [+0.68, +0.96] | +0.81 | +0.75 |
| cutoff 24, 25–34 | **+0.62** | −0.05 | −0.27 | −0.07 |
| cutoff 29, 30–39 | **+0.81** | +0.12 | +0.09 | +0.17 |
| cutoff 39, 40–42 (before) | **+0.99** | −0.01 | +0.01 | −0.02 |
| cutoff 39, 43–49 (after) | +0.31 [−0.09, +0.39] | +0.49 | +0.52 | **+0.53** |

Mean absolute recall error (lower is better):

| Run, window | S0 | S1 | S2 | S3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| cutoff 34, 43–49 (after) | 0.120 | **0.047** | 0.046 | 0.044 |
| cutoff 39, 43–49 (after) | 0.090 | 0.031 | **0.025** | 0.029 |
| cutoff 34, 35–42 (before) | **0.045** | 0.106 | 0.100 | 0.121 |

**The pattern:**
- **No shift (cutoffs 24, 29; before-shutdown windows):** recent data (S0) is the better stress test, often by a
  lot. Synthetic motif sets rank detectors at about ρ ≈ 0.
- **Across the dark-market shutdown (both cutoffs whose window contains step 43):** every synthetic motif stress test
  (S1, S2 and S3 alike) predicts post-shutdown catch rates **better** than recent data. The error is 0.04–0.05 against
  0.12 (cutoff 34; the CI of S1 − S0 excludes 0), and 0.03 against 0.09 (cutoff 39). At cutoff 39 the ranking is also better
  (S1 − S0 Δρ +0.18 [+0.08, +0.58]).
- **The likely mechanism** (per-detector recalls, cutoff 34):
  - *Linear detectors* (lr, lr_scaled) flag 24–29% of synthetic scenarios and keep 23–44% recall after the shutdown.
  - *Tree ensembles* (rf, hgb, extra_trees) flag only 3–5% of synthetic scenarios and collapse to 1–3% recall after the shutdown.
  - So unfamiliar shapes expose the same over-specialisation that the real shift does. This fits Maganti (2026): Elliptic's
    graph structure misleads under temporal shift.

**Sample size and multiplicity (added 2026-11-22):** the after-shift windows contain only **169** illicit transactions (steps 43–49), against 914 in 35–42. Four stress tests were compared across up to 7 windows, with no multiplicity correction. Both weaken the lead.

**Status of this pattern:** the post-shutdown window was pre-specified for reporting, and S0 is a pre-specified
comparison. But "synthetic motifs in general beat recent data after a shift" was **not** the pre-registered hypothesis,
which was about S1 specifically. It rests on one shift event, seen at two overlapping cutoffs. Treat it as a finding to
test prospectively, not a confirmed result.

## 3. E5: do the thresholds fixed before the cutoff hold up?

The target false-positive rate was 5%. Actual rates on licit transactions:

| Cutoff, window | Range | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 34, steps 35–49 | 0.2%–9.0% | rf 5.0%, hgb 5.1%, lr_scaled 9.0% |
| 29 | up to 15% | false-alarm cost drifts upward |
| 39 | about 0.5% | detectors go quiet after the shift |

## 4. The risk written down in advance, and what happened

The design warned that graph detectors might flag almost no synthetic scenarios, making E3 uninformative.
**Partly true:** mean synthetic recall is about 8–10%, and tree ensembles flag 1–5%. But because linear detectors flag
24–29%, the *spread* across detectors is wide enough for ranking to be informative (ρ up to 0.91).

## 5. Bottom line

1. **The project's specific hypothesis is not supported:** choosing scenarios with a literature-graph forecast does not beat
   choosing motif pairs at random.
2. **The forecasting step itself works** where the graph is sparse enough: a +0.241 AUC gain on the AML literature, and
   +0.049 on the physics control.
3. **A usable, testable lead:** synthetic motif stress tests beat recent data at predicting detector performance *after a
   shift*, and lose to it otherwise. A practical rule follows: **use recent data for routine monitoring, and use synthetic
   motif scenarios to estimate how detectors will hold up under a regime change.** This needs a second shift event
   (another dataset or a later period) to confirm.
